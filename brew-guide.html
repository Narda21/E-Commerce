YOUR CUP, YOUR WAY

You don't need a fancy setup. A few small adjustments to coffee amount, grind, water, and freshness can help you discover a cup you love.

CHOOSE YOUR METHOD

Three ways to get brewing.

Use these starting points, then adjust to taste. Different beans and equipment may need a little experimentation.

METHOD 01 · CLEAN & BRIGHT

Pour-over

Great for a clear cup where delicate flavors can stand out. Rinse the paper filter, add medium-fine grounds, and pour hot water slowly in circles.

  • Starting ratio: 15 g coffee to 250 g water
  • Grind: Medium-fine, like table salt
  • Try with: Highland Daybreak

Tip: Pour in stages and keep the grounds evenly wet rather than flooding one spot.

METHOD 02 · ROUND & COMFORTING

French press

An easy full-bodied method that needs little equipment. Add coarse grounds and hot water, stir gently, let steep, then press slowly.

  • Starting ratio: 18 g coffee to 270 g water
  • Grind: Coarse, like sea salt
  • Try with: Sunrise Blend

Tip: If the cup tastes muddy, try a coarser grind or pour it out soon after pressing.

METHOD 03 · CONCENTRATED

Espresso-style

Espresso uses pressure to make a small, concentrated drink. Home machines vary, so use the machine's recommended dose and adjust gradually.

  • Starting point: Follow your basket's dose range
  • Grind: Fine; adjust to flow
  • Try with: Night Shift

Tip: Change one variable at a time, such as grind size, so you can tell what helped.

KEEP IT FRESH

Protect the flavor between brews.

Roasted coffee gradually loses aroma and flavor. Store it well and buy an amount you can enjoy while it is fresh.

01

Choose an airtight container

Limit exposure to air and moisture. An opaque container also helps protect beans from light.

02

Keep it cool and dry

Store coffee away from direct sunlight, heat sources, moisture, and strong odors.

03

Grind close to brew time

Whole beans generally retain their aroma better than pre-ground coffee. Grind only what you plan to use.

04

Buy manageable amounts

Smaller bags can help you finish coffee while it still tastes lively, especially if you brew only a cup or two each day.

Learning references: National Coffee Association, “Storage and shelf life” for general storage guidance; About Coffee for an overview of common brewing methods. The ratios above are suggested demo starting points, not universal rules.
